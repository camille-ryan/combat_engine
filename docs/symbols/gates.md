# Gates -- the `when=` context

Almost every modifier takes `when=lambda ctx: ...`. This is the whole vocabulary a gate may read.

> Generated by `scripts/symbols.py`. Do not edit: `check.py` fails
> when a committed file and the code disagree. `--write` rewrites it.

## `when=` context keys

A gate is `when=lambda ctx: ...`. The ctx is a plain dict, so a key
that is not written is not an error -- the gate is simply never
true, and the row lays its effect and does nothing. `m5788a0` reads
`ctx["vs"]`, which nothing writes, so its immunity has never
applied (#591). `symbols.py` refuses a key that is not listed here.

- `ctx['ability']` -- which ability scored the roll.
- `ctx['action_point']` -- was an action point spent on this.
- `ctx['actor']` -- whoever is acting, on a ctx built from a non-attack event.
- `ctx['advantage']` -- did the attack have combat advantage.
- `ctx['ammo']` -- the ammunition used.
- `ctx['among']` -- the set a choice was made from.
- `ctx['arena_alt']` -- the OTHER arena weapons, as context substitutions -- a tuple of `{weapon, group}` dicts, empty for every creature that has not selected a pair and for every swing with something else.

Not a key a gate reads. It is read by `Mod.applies`, which re-asks a **failed** feat-kind gate on an attack or damage roll with each substitution laid over the context -- which is how `cf:fighter-weaponmaster-f3s0`'s printed *"any of your feats that grant feat bonuses to attack rolls or damage rolls with one of your arena weapons apply to your other arena weapon as well"* reaches a `when=` predicate written inside another feat's body. Nothing can reach into another row's predicate to widen it, so the widening happens where the predicate is read.

Written by `resolve._arena_alts` off `Gear.arena`, into both the attack context and the damage context. #1276.
- `ctx['as_']` -- the action it is being taken as.
- `ctx['attacker']` -- who is swinging. `target` is who it lands on.
- `ctx['branch']` -- which half of a melee-or-ranged row is in use.
- `ctx['by']` -- the **ref that opened the opportunity window**, on the ctx `c.no_provoke(when=)` is handed -- not on an attack ctx. `""` when a *move* opened it, the same convention `provoked_by` uses and what lets a row tell the two apart.

The same datum as `provoked_by` below, reached the same way and deliberately **not** the same key: these are two different contexts. `provoked_by` is on the ctx `resolve.attack` builds for the swing coming *through* the window; this is on the ctx the veto is asked *before* the window stands. Written by `Cast.no_provoke`'s veto off `getattr(ev, "by", "")`, which `dsl.use` sets as a plain attribute after construction -- `OpportunityWindow` has no such field, the way `Hit` has no `among` field.

Added for `arms.i468x1`, *"you do not provoke opportunity attacks for making ranged or area attacks with alchemical items"*: the ctx carried `why`, which is the same fact as prose (`"i1564p1 is a ranged power"`), and reading a ref back out of a sentence is the fragile shape this file exists to prevent. #1233.
- `ctx['charge']` -- is this use a charge.
- `ctx['conditions']` -- the conditions in play for this read.
- `ctx['crit']` -- was it a critical hit.
- `ctx['dtype']` -- the damage type of this blow.
- `ctx['dtypes']` -- every type of a blow that is several at once.
- `ctx['effect']` -- the effect being asked about.
- `ctx['from_attack']` -- did this come out of an attack.
- `ctx['gap']` -- what sort of hole a blocking square has -- "door", "cracked window". Read by `movement._may_squeeze` off `Grid.porous`, so a card naming one sort is a predicate on one mechanism rather than a second mechanism. #674.
- `ctx['granted_by']` -- who handed this creature the attack, or -1.
- `ctx['granted_via']` -- the ref of the row that handed it over -- an attack, or since #672 a **saving throw**. `Cast.save` writes it from its own `ref`, the way `Effect.by` does, and it is empty for the ordinary end-of-turn throw nobody granted.
- `ctx['group']` -- the `Weapon.group` of the weapon this swing went through, `""` for a swing through no weapon -- which is every implement power.

Both this and `weapon` below, because a card names one weapon as often as a family. `hand` answers a DIFFERENT question -- which of two **held** weapons swung -- so a trait requiring the off hand to be empty never sees a swing stamped `"off"`, which is `cf:fighter-weaponmaster-f3s2`'s worked case: it prints a bonus to *unarmed* attacks and the group is how an unarmed swing is told from an armed one.

Written by `resolve._group_of`, which looks it up on the attacker rather than taking it from the call site -- `Gear.weapons` holds per-character `replace(w)` copies, so it reads whatever `c.improvised` or `c.weapon_group` has done to the weapon. On the damage context too since #1276. #1276.
- `ctx['hand']` -- which hand the weapon is in.
- `ctx['how']` -- the kind of forced movement -- push, pull, slide.
- `ctx['keywords']` -- the keywords of the row or effect being asked about.
- `ctx['kind']` -- **which kind of movement is asking to enter the square** -- on the ctx a `c.may_enter(when=)` veto is handed, and on no other. One of `walk`, `shift`, `run`, `charge`, `teleport`; written by `movement._clear`'s `move={...}` beside `squares` and `by`.

Beside `squares` rather than folded into it because the two printed shapes ask different questions of one step: *"you can shift through an enemy's space"* is about the kind, and *"you can move through on a move of 3 or more"* is about the budget. A gate handed one could not write the other.

**The hazard this entry exists against**: a ctx is a plain dict, so before `move=` was merged in, a gate reading `ctx['kind']` was False on every step and the row did nothing -- which is this key's whole reason for being named here rather than left to be rediscovered.
- `ctx['label']` -- the label of the effect being asked about.
- `ctx['marker']` -- the creature trying to lay a mark, on the context `Cast.mark` builds to ask whether it is allowed to. Beside `target`, which is the creature it is trying to mark.

Two keys rather than one because all four printed lines forbidding a mark differ on *whom* it may not mark, and two of them scope it to a creature other than the caster -- `m115917a0` reads the marker's own enemies and `m4471a3` names the monster that hit it. A gate handed only the target could write neither. `Cast.cannot_mark` is the writer. #1312.
- `ctx['mode']` -- the movement mode in use.
- `ctx['move_kind']` -- how a creature moved -- `walk` `shift` `teleport` `push` `pull` `slide` `charge` `run`. Written by `Cast.does_not_provoke`'s window off `Moved.kind_`, and by `Cast._extra_every`. The event field keeps the trailing underscore because a field named `kind` shadows `Event.kind`.
- `ctx['mover']` -- who is trying to enter a square. `movement.may_enter`.
- `ctx['natural']` -- the d20 face of the check being resolved. Written by `skills.check` and `skills.ability_check` from inside their resolve callbacks, so it is **absent on a standalone read** of `skills.modifier` -- which is the right way round: a gate on the face is a contingency on a die that has not been thrown. It is the paying half of `CheckDeclared`, where the calling half happens. #693.
- `ctx['occupant']` -- who is standing in it. The other half of `mover`.
- `ctx['ongoing']` -- is this ongoing damage rather than a blow.
- `ctx['opportunity']` -- is this an opportunity attack.
- `ctx['origin']` -- the squares this swing comes **from**, as a frozenset. Not the attacker's square: a ranged attack's origin moves with `c.cast_from` or `c.cast_from_square`, and an area power's is the square it was **placed** at, which may hold no creature at all.

A set because an origin can be several squares -- a Large creature's footprint, or a shaman with two spirits lending at once. Empty when the caller said nothing, so a gate narrowing on it should fail closed rather than grant on a guess. `Cast._origin_squares` is what fills it; `p16653` reads it for "against attacks that originate outside the zone".
- `ctx['power']` -- the ref of the row doing this. The commonest gate.
- `ctx['provoked_by']` -- the **ref that opened the opportunity window** this swing is coming through, and `""` when a *move* opened it, because a move is not a row. Written by `resolve.attack` off `World.provocation`, which `c.provoke`, `dsl._survive_provoking` and `movement.step` each park their window on for the duration of its emit. Read only when `opportunity` is true, so an ordinary attack cannot inherit one. #880.
- `ctx['provoked_kind']` -- how the provoker was moving when it opened the window -- `movement.step`'s own `walk` / `shift` / `charge` -- and `""` when a row rather than a move opened it. **The absence is the signal**: it is what lets "provoked by this movement" be told from "provoked by using a ranged power", which `opportunity` alone could not. #301 put it on the event, #880 carried it here.
- `ctx['provoked_mode']` -- how the provoker was travelling -- walk, fly, swim, burrow. #880.
- `ctx['provoked_reach']` -- the **provoking** row's `Range.kind`, in `reach`'s own vocabulary, so "an opportunity attack you provoke when using a ranged or an area power" is one gate. Narrower than `provoked_by` being non-empty, which means only that *some* row opened the window -- `c.provoke` opens one for arbitrary rows. #880.
- `ctx['purpose']` -- what the skill check was FOR. THREE callers say, and this listed one: `movement.walk` passes "swim", `suffocation.check` passes "suffocation" (#1115) and `actions` passes `FEINT_PURPOSE` -- which was already true when the entry claimed "nothing else says anything today". Written by `skills.modifier`, off `SkillCheck.purpose`, which `skills.check` sets from its own argument. #1111.

It is the ONE narrowing three printed clauses need and could not have: `i2746x1`'s +2 to Athletics *made to swim* and its +2 to Endurance *made to hold your breath* -- both written now -- and a +5 to Perception *made to find hidden creatures* (`p13783:augment1`), which still waits. Laying any of them flat is a row stronger than its card. The attribute was announced on the event before this, so a row WATCHING a resolved check could read it (`f946`, on a Bluff made to feint) while a STANDING bonus could not -- the context is what `Mods.total` is handed and the key was not in it.

Empty for every caller that does not say, so a gate asking for a purpose is False on an ordinary check rather than absent from the context.
- `ctx['range_kind']` -- the shape of the attack -- `Range.kind` verbatim: `melee` `ranged` `close_burst` `close_blast` `area_burst` `wall`. Written by `dsl`'s stretch and reshape gates and by `resolve`'s ranged pass. Not the same key as `reach`, which `resolve` writes from `_reach_kind` on an attack's own context -- two spellings of a near-identical fact, left apart because they are written by different passes and read by different gates.
- `ctx['ranged']` -- was it a ranged attack.
- `ctx['reach']` -- the shape of the attack -- `Range.kind` verbatim.
- `ctx['reroll_by']` -- the ref of whatever asked for the reroll.
- `ctx['roll_kind']` -- which kind of roll is being changed -- `attack` `check` `save`. Written by `p6186`, the one row that boosts whatever triggered it, and read by the three rows that narrow its die to an attack roll. Not `Cast.boost_roll`'s `kind=`, which is a parameter and a filter.
- `ctx['skill']` -- the skill being checked, lowercased.
- `ctx['source']` -- who laid the effect.
- `ctx['squares']` -- **how many squares the movement has to spend**, on the same `c.may_enter(when=)` ctx as `kind` and written by the same `move={...}`. The budget the step is being paid out of, not the distance already covered.

See `kind` above for why both are carried.
- `ctx['target']` -- who it lands on. The commonest key in the tree.
- `ctx['weapon']` -- which weapon this swing went through, by ref, `""` for a swing through none. See `group` above for why both: a card names one weapon as often as a family, the same reason `query.holding` matches a ref and a group.

Written by `resolve.attack` off its own `weapon=` argument, which `Cast.attack` fills from `_swung_weapon` -- and that returns `""` unless the **declared row** carries the weapon keyword, so an implement power stamps nothing. On the damage context too since #1276: `deal_damage` had taken the parameter all along and never put it there. #1276.
- `ctx['when_']` -- where in the turn a saving throw was taken -- `"end"` for the ordinary one, `"early"` for one moved or granted at the start. Written by `Effects.save` and `Cast.save`, which take it as a keyword. Named with the trailing underscore for `Event.kind`'s reason: a field called `when` shadows the property `wire()` puts in every replay envelope. #671.
- `ctx['why']` -- the reason recorded for this.
